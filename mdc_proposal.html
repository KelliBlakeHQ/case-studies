The Engagements

Each talk is designed for its specific audience and moment — but both draw from the same body of lived and professional experience that makes Kelli uniquely positioned to speak to these rooms.

June 2026 MDC North
Campus
Caribbean American Heritage Month
“Defining the Caribbean Gaze™: How Creatives Turn Culture Into a Career”
A candid conversation about what it means to build a creative life rooted in Caribbean identity — and how to stop giving your culture away for free. Drawing from a lifetime of connection to Caribbean arts and culture, 25+ years of professional practice, and original field research conducted across the region, this talk reframes heritage not as background but as IP, voice, and competitive advantage.
Audience Caribbean creative students, artists, emerging founders
Format Fireside chat · 45–60 min
November 2026 MDC Idea
Center
Entrepreneurship Month
“Heritage as IP: How Cultural Founders Build Sponsorship-Ready Brands”
A practitioner's framework for turning cultural identity into fundable, licensable creative business. Drawing from original graduate research into the Caribbean creative economy and ongoing work with the Jamaica Screen Development Initiative, Sundance Institute, and Black Public Media, this session teaches founders how to price, pitch, and protect their cultural advantage — and how to open doors for Caribbean creatives at the highest levels.
Audience Student founders, creative entrepreneurs, cultural producers
Format Lecture + workshop · 60–90 min

Why MDC · Why Now

The Room Already Exists

Miami-Dade is the most Caribbean county in the United States

Over 862,000 Caribbean immigrants call Miami-Dade home — the highest concentration of any U.S. county. MDC's student body reflects that reality. These talks meet students exactly where they already are.

A Gap No One Has Filled

No institution runs a sustained Caribbean entrepreneurship program

No South Florida institution currently runs a sustained Caribbean entrepreneurship program inside a business or innovation context. Both engagements address that gap directly — with a speaker who has already built and tested the frameworks she will be teaching.

Every Seat, Occupied

Strategist, producer, researcher, door-opener

Kelli has sat in every seat — strategist, producer, advisor, sponsor liaison, festival executive, researcher, and door-opener. She brings tools students can use immediately, not after a graduate degree.

Embedded, Not Visiting

Actively embedded in this community

Through LVTY List and ongoing advocacy for Caribbean artists and institutions, Kelli remains actively embedded in this community — making both engagements a genuine contribution, not a one-time appearance.

About Kelli Blake

Generational. Structural.
Lived.

Kelli Blake is a Creative & Cultural Executive whose connection to Caribbean arts and culture is not measured in years — it is generational, structural, and lived. She has spent 25+ years building professional infrastructure at the intersection of strategy, storytelling, sponsorship, and cultural production.

01
The Carib Collective — Graduate Research, SCAD 2019
As the capstone of her M.A. in Business Design and Arts Leadership from the Savannah College of Art and Design, Kelli developed and launched The Carib Collective — a field-tested artist support program built on original focus group research conducted across the Caribbean. In 11 weeks, the pilot registered 222 emerging artists, reached an audience of 15.5 million, and raised 48% of its budget in under seven days. That research became the foundation for LVTY List.
02
LVTY List — The Pulse of Caribbean Creative Culture
Born from her graduate thesis research, LVTY List is Kelli's ongoing platform documenting and amplifying Caribbean creative culture — stewarding artists, surfacing opportunities, and building the connective tissue between the diaspora and the global creative industry.
03
Academic & Festival Credentials
Kelli has been trusted in academic settings before — returning to SCAD to guest lecture in the Creative Leadership program. She also served as a panelist at the 2024 Third Horizon Film Festival, the leading Caribbean cinema platform in the United States, held at the Pérez Art Museum Miami and Koubek Center.
04
Institutional Work
Kelli has worked with and alongside Sundance Institute (Industry Office), Black Public Media, Cleveland International Film Festival, Jamaica Screen Development Initiative, and IMAS — building the frameworks, partnerships, and audience experiences that move organizations from fragmented potential to executed impact.
